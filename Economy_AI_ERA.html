mAI Economy / Window Guidance as Code

Rediscovering finance with AI

A credit-based foundation for AI-assisted window guidance.

How does newly created credit become production, asset purchases, or financial fragility? We are building a framework to study that process and test how guidance can change it.

The research question

Start with credit creation

When a bank extends a loan by crediting an account, it creates a deposit as well as a debt. Lending is still constrained by demand, profitability, capital, and settlement needs. A useful model must follow what happens after the loan, including payments, repayment, and losses. [1]

We want to compare guidance rules within that process. A target for lending is not the same as realized lending: banks may adjust only partly, borrowers may change their plans, and finance may move outside the guidance perimeter. Japan's experience with window guidance makes this last problem especially important. [2]

Foundation

Keep the accounts. Test the mechanisms.

Credit as a set of relationships

The planned model records who lends to whom, on what terms, and for which evidenced use. Every payment has a counterpart. Credit for new production is distinguished from existing-asset purchases and refinancing; unknown use remains unknown.

AI-readable records will keep source data separate from assumptions about behavior and from policy objectives.

Thermo-Credit as a candidate state representation

Thermo-Credit asks whether a compact description of credit composition and stress preserves the information needed to compare interventions.

Its thermodynamic variables are candidates to test against ordinary balance-sheet measures. They are not established economic laws or validated safety thresholds.

Measurement work and earlier theory note

The role of AI

Develop hypotheses. Compare guidance.

In research, AI can propose competing mechanisms and help design tests that distinguish them. A story that fits a historical episode is a starting hypothesis, not a discovered law.

In a simulator, AI can propose guidance using the observations available at that time. We will compare it with both fixed rules and a transparent adaptive baseline, including cases where its model is wrong.

Institutions set the objectives and permitted actions. The system should show who gains, who bears risk, and which assumptions drive a recommendation. Those trade-offs remain subject to human review.

Available

Measurement code and research materials

Thermo-Credit provides regional dashboards, a measurement paper, and JSON / CLI / MCP interfaces. The Japanese measure describes borrower composition, not the final use of each loan. EU and US panels use coarser proxies.

The existing scenario tool applies specified shocks. It does not simulate banks responding to guidance.

Next experiment

A small credit-creation simulator

First, balance the accounts for lending, payment, repayment, and loss. Then compare no guidance, fixed guidance, and adaptive guidance under the same shocks.

Test weak compliance, funding substitution, and mistaken behavioral assumptions. Keep tuning and evaluation separate. Publish failures as well as gains.

Read the model and evaluation plan

If AI adds no benefit over a simpler rule, keep the simpler rule. If Thermo-Credit hides information needed for the decision, revise the representation. Simulation results alone will not establish real-world policy effects.

Licence expiry checks without ID images

Published October 4, 2026Updated October 17, 2026日本語

Check licence expiry without handing over a copy of the licence. ToppyMicroServices demonstrates how to do this without sharing the driver’s name or address.

Consider a car-sharing company in Japan checking a driver’s licence.

1. Recreate the police licensing role

The demo uses a test program to stand in for the police licensing side. It signs a record of the driving entitlement and expiry date so its source can be checked. The driver keeps this record.

2. The driver sends a proof

Using the record, the driver creates a proof that the licence covers ordinary cars and has not expired on the day of use. The company receives the proof without a licence image, name or address.

This uses zero-knowledge proofs, a cryptographic method for checking a condition without seeing the underlying information.

3. The company checks the proof

The company’s system verifies the proof and checks the required conditions. It does so without receiving the exact expiry date.

What the demo covers

This is a test-data demo. It is not connected to actual police systems and does not read real licences, authenticate drivers or check suspensions and revocations.

Technical verification details

The verifier uses the test issuer’s preconfigured public key and compares the proof’s conditions with its stored request. It rejects proofs made for weaker conditions and further submissions against a request whose proof has already been accepted.

In Japan, licences are issued by prefectural public safety commissions, with procedures handled at police licensing offices. Official guidance

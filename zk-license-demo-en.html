Receive proof of the required conditions
without collecting a licence copy

October 4, 2026

日本語で読む

ToppyMicroServices has published a demonstration for exploring how identity checks can collect and retain less information. Using fictional credentials, it verifies that an issuer has recorded an ordinary-car driving entitlement and an expiry date on or after the intended service date. The holder does not disclose their name, address or licence number for this check.

A question raised by a car-sharing data breach

On September 29, 2026, Park24 reported that identity-document information, including driving-licence images, had leaked for about 1.6 million Times Car accounts. This is an account count, not a count of licence images. Park24 announcement

The incident prompts a practical question: what information does a service need to receive and keep for each check? Toppy has not investigated the incident's intrusion path or storage arrangements. We do not claim that this demonstration would have prevented the reported harm.

Collect less information at the condition-checking step

If a check only needs to establish an expiry condition, receiving an entire image containing a name and address may be avoidable. In the demonstration, a trusted issuer signs a credential that the holder keeps. The holder generates a proof of the required conditions, and the verifier checks it against the issuer's signature rather than relying on self-reported values.

Zero-knowledge proofs and this general approach have prior work. This announcement is not a claim that Toppy invented a new cryptographic method. Our contribution is a small public implementation in which readers can inspect what the verifier receives and how it rejects invalid submissions. It uses existing AnonCreds technology.

The sample does not connect to real Japanese driving licences, check suspension or cancellation, or authenticate the person using it. It also does not imply that information needed for contracts, insurance or accident handling can be discarded.

Practical recommendations for retention and management

Organisations can consider the following steps without waiting for a new technology. These are Toppy's operational recommendations, not a universal retention period or an instruction to delete all existing records.

  • Record why data is kept and for how long. Treat document images, check results and contract information separately. Record their purpose, location, responsible owner and retention period. Where legal duties or accident and dispute handling require retention, record that reason too.
  • Choose the records each process needs. Consider whether an entire image is necessary, or whether a record of the check's time, conditions and result meets the purpose. Proofs and usage histories also need a defined purpose and retention period.
  • Limit who can access data and how. Consider role-based access, multi-factor authentication for administrators, encryption in transit and at rest, and controlled access to keys. Avoid unnecessary copies of identity documents in email, support records and logs.
  • Remove data when its retention reason ends. Define a deletion process that covers processors, copies and backup expiry. Decide how deleted records will be handled after a restore. During an incident investigation, preserve necessary evidence before deciding what to delete.

Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission guidelines address purpose-specific retention, security measures and efforts to erase personal data when it is no longer needed. Specific periods and retention duties depend on the business and records involved. PPC general guidelines, in Japanese, PPC security FAQ, in Japanese

Keeping credentials with the holder still leaves responsibilities. Issuers need to check the original information, holders need to protect their devices and secrets, and verifiers retain requests and check histories. The proposal changes where information is held and what is collected; it does not remove the need to manage it.

What is available, and what deployment would require

The published sample implements proof generation with fictional data, verification in a separate process and replay rejection. Tests passed locally on macOS and in Linux CI. We have no production adoption or measured reduction in breach harm to report.

A real car-sharing deployment would need legitimate issuer integration, person authentication, suspension and cancellation checks, and a design that meets applicable contractual and insurance requirements. The demonstration is a public reference for examining those requirements.
